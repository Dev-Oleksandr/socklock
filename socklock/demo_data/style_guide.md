# SockLock style guide (sample)

## Brand voice
- Friendly and a little cheeky. Never mock the viewer.
- Sounds like a real person filming on a phone (UGC), not like a TV commercial.
- One idea per line. Short, everyday words. Present tense. Say "you" more than "we".
- Concrete beats clever: "the dryer ate my sock" beats "laundry woes".

## Offer facts (the only claims you may use)
- Tiny magnets in the cuff snap each pair together, so pairs survive the wash and the drawer.
- 5 pairs for $29, then a refill every quarter (every 3 months). Cancel anytime.
- Free shipping in the US.
- Machine washable and dryer safe.

## Banned words and claims
- Banned words: revolutionary, game-changer, game changer, miracle, magic, cheap, guaranteed, forever, best ever, life-changing.
- No health or medical claims (no "improves circulation", no "therapeutic magnets").
- No fake urgency ("only today", "last chance") unless the context gives a real deadline.
- No competitor brand names.
- Meta ad policy: do not assert or imply personal attributes ("Are you lonely?", "Struggling with your weight?").
- No emojis in voiceover. No exclamation marks in on-screen text.

## Length limits
- hook (scene 1 voiceover): max 12 words. It must make sense in the first 3 seconds.
- Any other voiceover line: max 20 words.
- On-screen text: max 6 words per scene, ALL CAPS, no full stop at the end.

## Structure that works for us
- Scene 1: a relatable sock problem or a surprising visual.
- Scenes 2-3: show the magnets snapping, explain in one plain sentence.
- Last scene: the offer (5 pairs for $29) and a clear call to action ("Tap Shop now").
